💼 1. The Classical OLS Wage Determination Model Lecture Activity 1 & 5
[Actual Wage] = Base Minimum + [Education Rate] × [School Years] + [Experience Rate] × [Work Years] + [Unobserved Life Noise (uᵢ)]
$$WAGE_i = \beta_1 + \beta_2 EDUC_i + \beta_3 EXPER_i + u_i$$

Why the error noise \(u_i\) exists: Two workers with identical 12 years of education and 5 years of experience will never earn the exact same salary. Ambition, innate talent, lucky breaks, and survey rounding errors are absorbed by the disturbance term.

🥣 2. Ramsey RESET Test: Why Squaring \(\hat{Y}\) is a Stroke of Genius! CRITICAL EXAM QUESTION • 6M
[Residual eᵢ] = [Original Model Inputs] + δ₁ × [Fitted Wage Guess]² + δ₂ × [Fitted Wage Guess]³ + [vᵢ]
$$e_i = \alpha_1 + \alpha_2 X_{2i} + \alpha_3 X_{3i} + \delta_1 \hat{Y}_i^2 + \delta_2 \hat{Y}_i^3 + v_i$$
💡 The Genius of \(\hat{Y}^2\): If a model has 4 variables, squaring each \(X\) directly plus cross-products requires adding 14 new terms, obliterating degrees of freedom and inducing severe multicollinearity! Because \(\hat{Y}_i\) is already the optimal linear combination of all \(X\)'s, squaring \(\hat{Y}_i^2\) tests for general quadratic curvature across the entire model using just ONE single parameter (\(\delta_1\))!
🍲 3. White's Heteroskedasticity Auxiliary Regression (The Soup Kitchen) Topic 10, Slide 14
[Squared Residual eᵢ²] = α₀ + [Sales] + [ROE] + [Sales]² + [ROE]² + [Sales × ROE] + vᵢ
$$e_i^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 SALES_i + \alpha_2 ROE_i + \alpha_3 SALES_i^2 + \alpha_4 ROE_i^2 + \alpha_5 (SALES_i \times ROE_i) + v_i$$

The Auxiliary Soup Kitchen: Throw every linear vegetable, roasted squared vegetable, and interaction spice blend into the pot. If the regression fit \(n \cdot R^2\) is statistically significant, variance is NOT constant—heteroskedasticity is detected!

4 Reasons for Disturbance Term (O.R.M.I.)

Why does the disturbance term \(u_i\) exist in economic models? Test your recall before checking!

Can you state the 4 factors represented by O, R, M, and I?

O — Omission of minor variables: Models must remain parsimonious; thousands of minor factors cannot all be included.
R — Random human behavior: Human decisions are inherently non-deterministic; identical people make different choices.
M — Measurement errors: Survey data suffers from recall bias, rounding off hours, and income misreporting.
I — Imperfect functional form: Real economic relations are non-linear; linear models are convenient approximations.
MNEMONIC #2 • 4 MARKS Topic 11, Slide 14

4 DW Test Assumptions (I.N.A.N.)

What are the 4 strict prerequisites required for Durbin-Watson \(d\) to be valid?

I
N
A
N

Remember: The 2nd 'N' is the most dangerous examiner trap!

I — Intercept included: The regression equation MUST have an explicit constant term (\(eta_1\)).
N — Non-stochastic regressors: Explanatory variables are fixed in repeated sampling (strictly exogenous).
A — Autoregressive AR(1) only: Disturbances follow \(u_t = ho u_{t-1} + \epsilon_t\) (does not test higher-order lags).
N — No lagged dependent regressor: \(Y_{t-1}\) must NOT appear as an explanatory variable (biases \(d\) toward 2).

Matrix Singularity Explainer

OLS normal equations require computing \((X'X)^{-1}\). If \(\det(X'X) = 0\), division by zero crashes the estimator!
