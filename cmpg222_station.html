🛡️ The 5-Second Sanity Check (Saves 4 Marks)

In the semester test, students inverted the X and Y axes, producing a slope of 2 216. Always check: Slope × typical X ≈ typical Y.
✔ Right: \(3.57 \times 16500 = 58900 \approx 75000\) (plausible).
✘ Wrong: \(2216 \times 16500 = \text{R36.6 Million}\) (absurd!).

Chapter 6: Distance & Nearest Neighbour Engine

Based on Past Exam Memo Question 3 (Jane vs Runner 1 vs Runner 2)

Participant Age (Raw) Distance (Raw km) Age Z-Score (\(\mu=31.6, \sigma=2.881\)) Distance Z-Score (\(\mu=5.6, \sigma=2.881\))
Jane (Target) 32 5.0 +0.1388 -0.2083
Runner 1 30 5.0 -0.5554 -0.2083
Runner 2 34 6.0 +0.8331 +0.1388
Jane ↔ Runner 1 Distance
Nearest Neighbour 🏆
|0.1388 - (-0.5554)| + |-0.2083 - (-0.2083)| = 0.6942 + 0.0000 = 0.6942
Why nearest? Exact same distance run (-0.2083), minimal age delta.
Jane ↔ Runner 2 Distance
Further
|0.1388 - 0.8331| + |-0.2083 - 0.1388| = 0.6942 + 0.3471 = 1.0413
Why further? Both age and distance deviate from Jane's standardised coordinates.
⚠️ The Scale Bias Trap: If data is NOT standardised into z-scores, variables with large units (e.g. Salary in Rands) completely dominate variables with small units (e.g. Age in years), making nearest neighbour completely invalid.

Chapter 2: Skewness, Outliers & Descriptives

Camm 6e Ch 2.1–2.8
Distribution Skewness Decoder

Test how mean and median behave under economic asymmetry:

Mean > Median (Pulled Right)

Economic wealth and income are classic right-skewed examples. A few ultra-wealthy outliers inflate the mean, while the median represents the true middle observation.

Boxplot Outlier Limits (1.5 × IQR Rule)
IQR = Q3 - Q1 = 30
Lower Limit = Q1 - 1.5(IQR) = -5.0
Upper Limit = Q3 + 1.5(IQR) = 115.0

Any point outside [Lower, Upper] is flagged as an outlier.
