Study Unit 4 Methodology

Participatory Action Research (PAR) 4-Phase Spiral

Defined by Robin McTaggart (1989) with 16 Tenets. PAR is cyclical, self-reflective inquiry aimed at improving social practice rather than treating community members as passive subjects.

Phase 1 / 4
STAGE 1
Reflection

Identify shared concerns & problematic social conditions with participants.

STAGE 2
Planning

Co-construct a joint, context-appropriate action strategy.

STAGE 3
Action

Implement deliberate interventions in the field with local actors.

STAGE 4
Observation

Systematically monitor real effects and feedback to begin next cycle.

Study Unit 1 Epistemology

The Knowledge Epistemology Matrix

Comparing Western/Scientific paradigms with Indigenous/Local frameworks (Michael & Gayton, 2002; Sillitoe, Bicker & Pottier, 2002).

Dimension Traditional / Indigenous Knowledge Scientific / Western Knowledge
Methodology & Data Mainly qualitative, experiential, holistic, long-term oral observation Mainly quantitative, reductionist, experimental, codified data
Epistemology & View Intuitive, spiritual, moral dimension embedded in ecosystems Rational, secular, value-neutral detachment from objects
Transmission & Storage Oral tradition, songs, storytelling, rituals, living practice Written texts, digital databases, scientific peer-reviewed journals
Ownership & Rights Communal, intergenerational, custodial responsibility, non-alienable Individual copyright, patents, limited term (20 yrs), commodity
Geographic Context Deeply rooted in local ecological niche and ancestral territory Universal claims, abstract, context-independent general laws
⚖️
The Incommensurability Debate (Michael & Gayton, 2002): They argue that because the two systems originate from vastly distinct worldviews and cultural frameworks, direct hierarchical comparison is flawed. Instead of treating IK as "primitive science" to be validated by laboratories, treat it as an autonomous, valid system of knowing.
Exam Marking Blueprint

Ms. C. Firkins' Long Question Marking Anatomy

Extracted directly from INL270_2026_ExamDetails&Info.pdf (Slides 11–13). Single-paragraph long answers will lose massive marks. Follow this exact 3-tier sequence.

🚫 Never 1 Unbroken Paragraph
Tier 1: Roadmap Introduction (1–2 Sentences) Order of Thoughts

"This essay examines the ethical and legal violations that occurred during the Havasupai genetic study at Arizona State University. Specifically, it identifies the failure of Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC), discusses the spiritual disruption caused to tribal ancestral narratives, and provides concrete remedies enacted through the 2010 legal settlement."

✅ Why it scores: Tells the marker immediately where the marks lie and establishes logical flow.
Tier 2: The Mandatory 3-Phase Body Paragraph Identify → Discuss → Example
1. IDENTIFY

Explicitly name the concept or violation (e.g. "Secondary research without Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC)").

2. DISCUSS / DESCRIBE

Unpack why this occurs theoretically, how Western science treats samples as raw data, and why this breaches communal trust.

3. CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Ground it in empirical reality (e.g. "ASU blood samples consented for diabetes were repurposed for schizophrenia and inbreeding").

Tier 3: Separate Concluding Paragraph Synthesis & Takeaway

"In conclusion, the case illustrates that genomic research cannot decouple physical biological data from the sacred worldview of indigenous custodians. True research partnership mandates ongoing consent, community ownership, and benefit-sharing protocols."
